29 of 44 configurations cannot separate their closest two members by 16 ulp.

Their ranking of those members is decided by rounding, so the update is not
reproducible across anything that perturbs the scores: a different GPU, a
newer XLA, TF32 instead of fp32. One swap is worth ~1e-2 in the update.

`centered_ranks` gives exactly tied members the same weight since the midrank
change, so an exact tie is no longer a source of disagreement. A pair one ulp
apart still gets distinct ranks and can still swap, and that is most of the
count above: the `close` column is what to read, not `ties`.

Remedies, in the order they were measured rather than the order they occur:
  - Raising sigma works when N is small and does NOT work when N is large.
    lowrank_r1 at d=256 N=64 goes 0 -> 56 ulp from sigma 0.01 -> 0.03, but
    iid_gaussian at d=256 N=256 goes 1 -> 8 -> 6 -> 2 ulp across 0.01 -> 0.3,
    because the loss magnitude grows with sigma and takes the ulp with it.
  - The binding constraint is N against float32 resolution. Separating N
    members by m ulp needs a relative loss spread of about N*m*eps, which is
    5e-4 at N=256, m=16. Large populations are the hard case, and this sweep
    goes to N=1024.
  - Continuous shaping measures 25x amplification against the rank
    transform's 2.65e5, so it does address near-ties. It is a different
    optimizer, not a free improvement: rank shaping is what buys robustness
    to outliers and to reward scale.
  - Or keep the configuration, pin the arithmetic, and state in the
    limitations that invariance holds under fixed arithmetic only.
