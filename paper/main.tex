\begin{abstract}
Evolution strategies (ES) are often described as cheap to distribute because
workers exchange scalar fitnesses rather than gradients, but they must still
place the population-weighted update contraction. We compare gathering
fitnesses and repeating the contraction on every device with contracting local
population shards and all-reducing model-sized partial updates. In a common
implementation of seed-regenerated full-rank and never-materialized low-rank
perturbations, we measure where each placement wins on an 8$\times$A100 node and a TPU
v5e-8, one host each, the fattest interconnects available. On those fabrics
all-reduce wins every measured dense and seed-regenerated cell, whereas
repeated contraction wins when low-rank structure makes the local work small.
The hardware-dependent outcome recurs with both signs on Qwen2.5-0.5B.

We separately test whether the faster perturbations preserve useful update
directions. Synthetic measurements yield an approximately square-root law for
cosine alignment with the true gradient. Across 0.5B- and 1.5B-parameter
models, it predicts full-rank teacher-forced-NLL alignment within 10\%; the
low-rank family lands near half its raw prediction, a factor with a quarter's
scatter of its own that carries from 0.5B to 1.5B within that scatter.
On decoded-reward Countdown, three seeds resolve no quality difference among
full rank and ranks 1, 4, and 16 at equal training samples, while rank 1 reduces
steady-state update time by 41\%. We release the code, configurations, and
results.
\end{abstract}
