\begin{abstract}
Two 2025 papers made evolution strategies work at LLM scale with opposite
structural bets: full-rank perturbations regenerated from seeds, and
rank-$r$ factored perturbations that are never materialized. Between them
sits a set of widely repeated but unmeasured beliefs: that ES is cheap to
distribute because only scalars cross the wire, that the low-rank rewrite
pays because GPUs are bad at dense noise, and that ES does not need
reinforcement learning's stabilizers. We measure all three, on an
8$\times$A100 node and a TPU v5e-8, with an open-source instrument that
expresses both bets in one system. The scalar claim is true and beside
the point: the update placement that all-reduces a model-sized buffer
wins much of the measured grid anyway, and where each placement wins
moves with the accelerator. The low-rank motivation strengthens on TPU
rather than inverting. The only synchronization point is a scalar
gather; the barrier above it is a replicated local sort. On a
0.5B-parameter task the cheap perturbation family matches full-rank
quality at equal samples with a $1.7\times$ accelerator-time advantage, as
the fitted estimator-quality curves predicted. Against true gradients on
real models at two sizes, the fit predicts full-rank cosine
within 10\%, its square-root population scaling transfers unrefitted, and a
correction calibrated at 0.5B predicts 1.5B low-rank cosine within 26\%.
The contraction crossover also reappears on the production model with
both signs intact. The stability folklore narrows to the learning-rate
margin, with a measured
parameter-drift cost reported beside it. Every result traces to a committed
script, configuration, and result record in the released repository.
\end{abstract}
