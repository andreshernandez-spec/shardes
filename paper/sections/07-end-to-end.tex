\section{Accuracy and task validation}
\label{sec:endtoend}

This section asks whether the systems savings preserve useful update
directions and task quality. The objectives form a deliberate chain rather
than one continuous experiment. The synthetic study uses mean-squared error;
the real-model alignment studies use teacher-forced negative log-likelihood
(NLL), whose exact gradient is available; and the training campaign optimizes
a nondifferentiable reward from decoded Countdown answers. Thus the alignment
experiments test whether a prediction transfers from a synthetic block to a
real language-model loss. The task campaign is a subsequent outcome check, not
a direct true-gradient validation of the decoded-reward objective.

\subsection{Synthetic alignment law}
On the synthetic block of Section~\ref{sec:setup}, we measured cosine between
the centered-rank ES update and the exact MSE gradient, with 30 replicates per
cell over 456 cells. Per-strategy fits of
$\log\cos = a + b\log(N/d_{\mathrm{samp}})$ have slopes from 0.494 to
0.508 (Figure~\ref{fig:f5}). That exponent is the classical
Gaussian-smoothing scaling, $\cos \propto \sqrt{N/d}$
\citep{nesterov2017,duchi2015}, confirmed rather than discovered; what the
measurement adds is that it survives centered-rank shaping, which changes
the estimand (Section~\ref{sec:background}), to within $0.01$ of one half.
At matched population, mirrored low-rank and
mirrored full-rank have similar alignment: the low-rank curves have lower
intercepts against $N/d_{\mathrm{samp}}$, while their smaller sampling
dimension shifts a fixed-$N$ comparison to the right.

Before the task campaign ran, evaluating these fits at its operating point
predicted no rank ordering that three seeds were likely to resolve: cosine
$1.6\times10^{-4}$ for full rank and $2.7$--$2.8\times10^{-4}$ for ranks 1
and 4. This was a long extrapolation---one to three decades below the smallest
measured $N/d_{\mathrm{samp}}$, from a synthetic MSE block to a real model.
Rank 16 was inferred only from the rank-1/rank-4 trend because the synthetic
study did not measure it.

\subsection{Transfer to language-model NLL}
We tested that extrapolation on the teacher-forced NLL of Qwen2.5-0.5B. For
each cell, five perturbation seeds produced one full ask--NLL--tell update,
which was compared with the exact NLL gradient (Table~\ref{tab:tb4}). The
full-rank measurements land within 10\% of the synthetic prediction. All
low-rank measurements fall below it in the same direction, but the cell ratios
range from $0.43$ to $0.67$ rather than forming an exact constant; their
geometric mean is 0.53. At $N{=}30$ the cross-rank ranges overlap; no
inferential test is claimed. Increasing $N$ moves every arm by roughly the square-root scale,
and at $N{=}240$ the unpaired rank-1 arm has $1.36\times$ the alignment of
mirrored rank 1, close to the synthetic ordering.

Two held-out probes test whether the remaining correction is portable.
Repeating the full-rank and rank-1 cells on a freshly drawn frozen prompt batch
reproduces their ratios to two decimals ($0.94\times$ and $0.52\times$ on
either batch). On Qwen2.5-1.5B, with predictions frozen before the run, the
within-arm $N$-scaling is $2.80\times$ and $2.94\times$ against
$\sqrt{8}=2.83$, and the raw full-rank prediction remains within 5--8\%.
Multiplying the rank-1 fit by the 0.53 correction measured at 0.5B predicts
the 1.5B cells within 23--26\%; the observed correction itself moves to about
0.40, so it is useful across this scale step but not scale-invariant
(Table~\ref{tab:tb6}). The correction is an empirical patch for a variable
that is not quite right: $d_{\mathrm{samp}}$ counts draws, and the
cell-to-cell scatter of the factor ($0.43$--$0.67$, about a quarter either
side of its mean) is as large as the $23$--$26\%$ it then predicts within,
so the defensible statement is that the low-rank family lands near half its
raw prediction, drifting with scale. Deriving the variable that absorbs the
factor is future work. Within these models, this objective, and the measured
populations and ranks, perturbation rank does not dominate alignment at equal
$N$. That is consistent with---but does not by itself explain---the task-level
parity tested next.

\subsection{Decoded-reward task validation}
\paragraph{The task, and what was fixed in advance.} The campaign that
tests its task-level implication is specified in Table~\ref{tab:e2e}: both algorithm
families train on the same puzzle pool with the published core
hyperparameters of \citet{qiu2025} (one disclosed budget difference:
8 puzzles per update against their protocol's 200 training samples), are
scored on the same 2000 disjoint held-out puzzles by greedy decode, and
stop at the same total budget of scored training completions, which is
the unit the x-axis of Figure~\ref{fig:f7} counts.

\paragraph{Task quality across ranks.} At population $N{=}30$, held-out
performance shows no detectable difference across full-rank and
rank-\{1,4,16\} perturbations in this three-seed experiment
(Figure~\ref{fig:f7}); no inferential test is claimed at $n{=}3$. Two
facts bound what that null can mean. The setup does resolve a known-worse
arm: the $4\times$ noise-scale ES arm of the appendix stress test stays at
the base-model floor for its whole 150-step horizon
(Appendix~\ref{sec:stability}). It does not resolve a modestly worse one.
As a positive control, the full-rank and rank-1 recipes were rerun at
population 16 on the same 120{,}000-sample budget, an arm the square-root
law predicts to align 27\% worse per update (prediction frozen in the
configuration before the run); it reaches the same plateau, inside seed
noise (Figure~\ref{fig:f7c}, Appendix~\ref{sec:validation}). Every arm
saturates by 24{,}000--36{,}000 samples and freezing 27\% of the
parameters changes nothing either. The null is therefore no detectable
difference in a setup whose ceiling is set by the task: it resolves a
freeze and does not resolve a 27\% alignment change, so it cannot rank
the perturbation families more finely than that. The
outcome agrees with the alignment study's pre-campaign prediction that the
ranks would be closer than a three-seed experiment could resolve, and says
nothing stronger. Because the
training objective is decoded reward rather than NLL, this non-difference is
consistent with the alignment result, not a direct test or proof of its
mechanism.

\paragraph{Equal samples, unequal seconds.} Figure~\ref{fig:f7}
resolves no rank-dependent quality difference over three seeds;
Figure~\ref{fig:f7b} prices the same curves in accelerator time. A
steady-state full-rank update costs 4.44\,s against 2.99\,s for rank 1,
means over the 498 steady-state updates each run observed (the
regeneration compute the cost surfaces of Section~\ref{sec:results}
price at microbenchmark scale, here on the real model): 33\% less time per
update, equivalent to a $1.50\times$ update-throughput advantage, and an
estimated 2211\,s against 1490\,s for a
500-update run. Time to first reach the shared plateau (held-out reward
0.15) is 432--1103\,s for full rank against 299--592\,s for rank 1
across seeds; the ranges overlap between 432 and 592\,s, so three seeds
do not resolve a strict time-to-threshold ordering, and the per-update
ratio is the clean claim. The rank-1 figure is for the program as released,
which pads the rank-1 factors to a rank-2 contraction so the TPU keeps
them fused; on the A100 that pad costs rank 1 16\% per update (a same-host
probe puts the unpadded program at 2.49\,s against 2.89\,s, archived with
the campaign), so a platform-specific build would widen the gap to 42\%.
This is where the systems and quality halves meet: the cheap perturbation
uses a third less time per update, while the experiment resolves no
additional sample requirement.

\paragraph{GRPO context.} The GRPO arm \citep{deepseekmath2024} uses the
published settings of \citet{qiu2025}, without tuning. Its variance across
three seeds and its decoder-specific floor preclude a controlled quality
comparison with ES; it is retained in Figure~\ref{fig:f7} only to show the
reference recipe at the same training-sample budget. A separate,
exploratory stress test of the folk belief that ES needs none of this
recipe's stabilizers, with its criteria fixed before the data and its scope
stated, is reported in Appendix~\ref{sec:stability}; it is comparative
optimizer robustness, not part of the systems argument.
