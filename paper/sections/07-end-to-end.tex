\section{End-to-end validation}
\label{sec:endtoend}

% ORDER (revised 2026-08-21, second review): determinism, then the synthetic
% estimator result, then the task prediction and its outcome, then the GRPO
% baseline, then stability. The argument is prediction-before-campaign, so the
% prediction's evidence now comes before the campaign's.
This section is the validity chain the introduction promised, on a real
model (Qwen2.5-0.5B on the Countdown task) with the same instrument, in
an order where each claim rests on the one before it. First,
reproducibility: the systems prices of Section~\ref{sec:results} are
worth acting on only if what was measured is the algorithm, so the
determinism boundary is stated exactly. Second, quality: the estimator
study's prediction, then the campaign that tests it, closing the
unmeasured half of the low-rank belief. Third, stability: the one folk
claim that cannot exist on a microbenchmark, measured last because its
verdicts lean on both of the claims before it.

\paragraph{Determinism and invariance on real hardware.} For a fixed seed and
compiled program, the full training step is bitwise reproducible across
processes on an 8-GPU node, and the update computed on 1 and 8 devices agrees
to $6\times10^{-6}$ relative error under a fixed set of fitnesses. Trajectory
equality across \emph{different} compiled programs is explicitly not claimed:
greedy decoding flips near-ties under different compiler fusion choices, and
we document the boundary rather than promise across it.

\paragraph{But is the fast strategy accurate?} The speed results of
Section~\ref{sec:results} would mean little if the low-rank rewrite bought
its speed with estimation quality, so the accuracy chain is stated in
full, with its methods. The estimator study measured, on the
synthetic block of Section~\ref{sec:setup}, the cosine between the ES
update direction and the exact gradient from automatic differentiation,
30 replicates per cell over 456 cells, and fitted
$\log\cos = a + b\log(N/d_{\mathrm{eff}})$ per strategy; every fitted
slope is one half (Figure~\ref{fig:f5}).
Per sample, on those objectives, mirrored low-rank matches mirrored
full-rank almost exactly at matched population: each low-rank sample loses
about $2.2\times$ to projecting the gradient through thin factors, and
low-rank's smaller effective dimension wins almost exactly that factor back
(the cancellation behind Figure~\ref{fig:f5}). Those fitted curves,
evaluated at the task's operating point before the campaign ran, put all
ranks within noise of each other (predicted cosines $1.7\times10^{-4}$
full against $2.7$--$2.8\times10^{-4}$ for ranks 1 and 4). The
extrapolation is far and is stated as one: one to three decades below the
smallest measured $N/d_{\mathrm{eff}}$, from a synthetic block to a real
model, with rank 16 riding on the rank-1/rank-4 trend because the
estimator study never measured it. That extrapolation is now measured
rather than trusted. On Qwen2.5-0.5B itself, the cosine between one
production update and the true gradient of the same teacher-forced loss
(five seeds per cell, Table~\ref{tab:tb4}) lands within 10\% of the
fitted prediction for full rank, and uniformly at about half of it for
the low-rank family (ratios $0.43$--$0.67\times$ across ranks and
populations): the real transformer's loss surface charges roughly twice
what the synthetic block did for the low-rank projection, as one
systematic factor. Because the shortfall is uniform, everything relative
transfers to the real model: the cross-rank tie at $N{=}30$, the
square-root scaling in $N$ inside every arm, and the unpaired scheme's
per-sample advantage, predicted at $1.34\times$ and measured at
$1.36\times$. The mechanism's reading: in the LLM-ES
regimes studied here and in the source papers, the population is
minuscule against the parameter count, and in that regime the rank of the
perturbation is not what limits estimation quality. That, arguably, is
\emph{why} the low-rank bet can work at all.

tests the prediction is specified in Table~\ref{tab:e2e}: both algorithm
families train on the same puzzle pool with the published core
hyperparameters of \citet{qiu2025} (one disclosed budget difference:
8 puzzles per update against their protocol's 200 training samples), are
scored on the same 2000 disjoint held-out puzzles by greedy decode, and
stop at the same total budget of scored training completions, which is
the unit the x-axis of Figure~\ref{fig:f7} counts.

\paragraph{Task quality across ranks.} At population $N{=}30$, held-out
performance shows no detectable difference across full-rank and
rank-\{1,4,16\} perturbations in this three-seed experiment
(Figure~\ref{fig:f7}); no inferential test is claimed at $n{=}3$. The
outcome is the one the estimator study's fitted curves predicted, which
put all ranks within noise of each other at this
population-to-effective-dimension ratio; a non-difference is consistent
with the estimator mechanism, not proof of it.

\paragraph{Equal samples, unequal seconds.} Figure~\ref{fig:f7}
establishes equal sample efficiency across ranks; Figure~\ref{fig:f7b}
prices it. A steady-state full-rank generation costs 4.3\,s against
2.4\,s for rank 1 (the regeneration compute the cost surfaces of
Section~\ref{sec:results} price at microbenchmark scale, here on the
real model), so the same 500 generations take 2785\,s against 1594\,s,
and the time to first reach the shared plateau (held-out reward 0.15)
is 1041--1745\,s for full rank against 623--897\,s for rank 1 across
seeds: disjoint ranges, about $1.7\times$ less accelerator time for the
same quality. This is where the systems and quality halves meet: the
cheap perturbation reaches matched quality in $1.7\times$ less training
time precisely because Figure~\ref{fig:f7} shows it needed no more
samples.

\paragraph{The reference baseline, honestly.} GRPO
\citep{deepseekmath2024} with the published hyperparameters of
\citet{qiu2025}, untuned in either direction: two of three seeds plateau
early at 4.9\% of held-out puzzles solved, and one never solves a
held-out puzzle, flat at format-only reward from step 50. None falls
below its decoder's base-model floor, so none meets the collapse
definition the stability section uses; the word collapse is reserved for
runs that do. We report this as seed variance in the reference, not as a
victory for ES; the figure's role is that both algorithm families run end
to end in comparable budgets, not a leaderboard.

\subsection{Stability under perturbation}
The stabilization machinery of deep RL exists for stated reasons, and the
recipe tested here carries two pieces of it: a clipped surrogate objective
against provable step-size limits \citep{kakade2002,schulman2015trpo,schulman2017ppo}
and a reference-model KL penalty anchoring the policy \citep{ouyang2022}.
(The value-based lineage's target networks and weight averaging, which
answer divergence theorems
\citep{baird1995,tsitsiklis1997,mnih2015,vanhasselt2018,zhang2021target},
are not part of this recipe and are not tested.) ES carries none of that
machinery, so we asked whether its absence is load-bearing, with metrics
and falsification criteria fixed before the data. The answer has two
halves (Figure~\ref{fig:f8}).

First, the half we expected, and the one matched comparison in the
experiment: multiplying the learning rate by 8 destroys GRPO on every
seed (reward 0.000, below even format compliance, policy entropy zero)
while the same multiplier leaves every ES seed at or above its
published-settings reference; neither algorithm collapses at $1/8\times$,
so the finding is about the aggressive endpoint of the tested 64-fold
interval, whose interior was not explored. Descriptively, and not as a
matched rate (the twelve perturbed runs per algorithm perturb different
knobs per family): zero ES collapses against three GRPO collapses, and
the one ES failure (noise scale $4\times$) is a freeze at the base-model
floor.

Second, the half our own falsification criteria caught: removing GRPO's KL
anchor entirely, or widening its clip until it never binds, changed little
at this horizon on this rule-based verifier. The single stabilizers are not
individually load-bearing here; the fragility is concentrated in the
learning rate. That is consistent with prior reports that clipping is not
what makes PPO work \citep{engstrom2020,andrychowicz2020} and that
critic-based PPO trains without a KL penalty \citep{orz2025},
and it narrows our claim to what the data supports.

Stability is also not free. The drift row of the same experiment shows the
$8\times$-learning-rate ES runs ending roughly $50$ units of parameter
$\ell_2$ from the pretrained weights, against $0.3$--$2$ for healthy GRPO
runs: ES tolerates aggressive steps by moving far, which is exactly the
regime where ES-induced forgetting has been reported
\citep{abdi2026forgetting}. Anyone citing this
section's stability numbers should read them next to that row.
