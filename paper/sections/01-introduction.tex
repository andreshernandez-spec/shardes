\section{Introduction}

Evolution strategies train a neural network without computing gradients: perturb
the weights with random noise, evaluate each perturbed copy, and move the weights
toward the copies that scored well (a zeroth-order, or gradient-free, method).
Its sample efficiency was long reported as several times worse than
gradient-based training \citep{salimans2017,such2017}, which kept it away
from large models. In 2025, two
papers changed that assessment, and they did it with opposite structural bets.
\citet{qiu2025} perturb every weight independently and avoid storing the noise by
regenerating it from random seeds (full-rank perturbations with seed
regeneration). Sarkar et al.'s EGGROLL \citep{eggroll2025} never builds the full noise matrix at
all: it perturbs each weight matrix with the product of two thin matrices (a
rank-$r$, or low-rank, factorization), which turns the expensive per-copy work
into a small correction on top of one shared matrix multiply.

Between and beneath those two papers sits a set of beliefs that we are
unaware of prior work measuring:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{``ES is cheap to distribute because only scalars cross the
    wire.''} Whether that is true depends on how the weight update is assembled
    from the evaluated candidates, a choice with two natural placements (we
    call them contraction strategies A and B, Section~\ref{sec:contraction}).
    One of them does move only scalars; the other all-reduces a buffer the
    size of the model.
  \item \emph{``The low-rank rewrite pays because GPUs are bad at the dense
    case.''} EGGROLL's motivation is explicitly GPU-shaped; on a TPU, whose
    matrix unit makes dense multiplies comparatively cheap, the balance could
    plausibly invert (Section~\ref{sec:results}). And a price comparison
    between perturbation families is only meaningful if the cheap family
    estimates as well as the expensive one, which is its own unmeasured
    half (Section~\ref{sec:endtoend}).
\end{itemize}

The two beliefs are systems questions; the second carries the quality
requirement just stated. That fixes the paper's shape. The beliefs are priced on a
controlled microbenchmark (Sections~\ref{sec:contraction}
and~\ref{sec:results}), where every byte and millisecond is attributable.
Those prices then need a validity chain before they are worth acting on,
and Section~\ref{sec:endtoend} is that chain, on a real model with the
same instrument: first that the measurements are of the algorithm rather
than of scheduling noise (reproducibility and device-count invariance on
hardware), then that the fast bet does not buy its speed with estimation
or task quality (the accuracy chain, closing the requirement). A third
folk belief, that ES does not need the stabilizing machinery
reinforcement learning carries, is a question about comparative
optimizer robustness rather than about distributed ES; it cannot be
measured on a microbenchmark at all, and we report an exploratory
stress test of it in Appendix~\ref{sec:stability}, scoped to what one
task, one recipe, and three seeds can say.

Measuring any of these fairly has a prerequisite that explains why it had not
happened: the two bets lived in incompatible codebases, so any comparison
confounded the algorithm with its implementation, and the incumbent library
(evosax; \citealp{lange2022evosax}) forecloses both in its core path, which
materializes the population as one dense (population, parameter-count)
matrix: that makes the factored bet inexpressible without bypassing the
API's central object, and the full-rank bet memory-infeasible at LLM
scale. We therefore built the
instrument first: \textbf{shardes}, an open-source JAX \citep{jax2018} library whose core
splits the population of perturbed model copies across devices, with the
perturbation scheme as a pluggable strategy, so both bets and their
combinations run in one system, which shrinks rather than eliminates the
implementation confound: each strategy keeps its own code path, but all of
them share the mesh, the evaluation loop, and the update machinery. The library is released and citable;
this paper is about what it measured.

\paragraph{Contributions, in order of strength.} The primary
contribution is the contraction study: where each update placement wins,
measured on both accelerators. Secondary is the instrument result, that
one abstraction expresses both published perturbation families at
competitive throughput (Table~\ref{tab:tb1}). The end-to-end experiments
are validation and exploration, not co-equal pillars: the training campaign
uses one model size, while the true-gradient check spans two. They are the
links of the validity chain just described, closed by a three-seed quality
check; the robustness stress test is exploratory and lives in the appendix.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The scalar folk claim is true and beside the point.} Strategy
    A does move only scalars (measured: twice the naive byte count,
    Table~\ref{tab:tb2}); strategy B moves a model-sized buffer every update
    and \emph{wins} every dense and seed-regenerated cell anyway, while the
    cost of that transfer is what returns the low-rank cells to A. Where
    each wins forms a
    crossover in (population, model size) space that moves between an
    8$\times$A100 node and a TPU v5e-8 (Section~\ref{sec:results},
    Figure~\ref{fig:f2}), in the direction predicted before the
    measurements; the byte model itself was corrected by them
    (Section~\ref{sec:contraction}, Table~\ref{tab:tb2}). Both signs also
    reappear when the crossover is measured on Qwen2.5-0.5B itself
    (Table~\ref{tab:tb5}).
  \item \textbf{The low-rank motivation does not invert on TPU; it
    strengthens.} At matched shapes, rank-1 perturbations cost
    $0.05$--$0.18\times$ the dense baseline on the v5e against
    $0.13$--$0.42\times$ on the A100, and the dense baseline is infeasible
    over most of the 16\,GB grid besides (Figure~\ref{fig:f4}).
  \item \textbf{The only synchronization shared by both contraction
    placements is the fitness gather, and the expensive part of the
    barrier above it is not communication.} The gather costs
    2.4--6\,$\mu$s at every measured population and device count; the
    sort behind centered-rank shaping (a property of the shaping, not of
    ES) costs 12\,ms at $N{=}2^{18}$ and is identical on 1 and 8
    devices: replicated local compute. Strategy B adds the one
    synchronization beyond these, its model-sized all-reduce.
  \item \textbf{Exploratory, in the appendix: a stress test of the
    stability folklore}, with criteria pre-specified in a committed
    protocol. The RL
    baseline collapses to zero on every seed at $8\times$ its published
    learning rate while ES shows no collapse at either endpoint of the
    same 64-fold interval; our own falsification criteria caught the part of the claim
    that was wrong, since removing the baseline's individual stabilizers
    changed little. One task, one recipe, three seeds; a stress test,
    not a margin (Appendix~\ref{sec:stability}, Figure~\ref{fig:f8}).
  \item Every figure and generated table regenerates from a committed script;
    every reported result traces to a committed configuration, result record,
    and recorded environment in the released repository.
\end{itemize}
