\subsection{Baselines}
\input{generated/tb1}
Matched shapes, matched populations, tokens per second; nothing here claims
step-equivalence between implementations. The arms differ in antithetic
pairing, shaping and normalization details by design, and the honest unit of
comparison is throughput at the same shapes, which is how both papers report.

Beyond parity, the measurements locate three concrete reasons to prefer the
instrument over the reference implementations, stated with their caveats.
First, none of the references shards: at eight devices the instrument reaches
$6\times$ the reference's single-chip ceiling (Table~\ref{tab:tb1b}), which
is a statement about released code, not about what the papers' internal
systems could do. Second, choices the references hardcode are measured to
matter: the contraction placement flips winner by platform
(Figure~\ref{fig:f2}), and EGGROLL's antithetic pairing is built into its
sampler, while the unpaired variant it cannot express estimates the gradient
about $1.34\times$ better per sample at matched population in the large-$N$
regime its own paper targets (the phase-0 curves behind
Figure~\ref{fig:f5}); the pairing halves factor storage in exchange, and at
small $N$ the difference is within noise, so this is a choice to expose, not
a defect. Third, the reference's rank-1 path does not compile on a 16\,GB
TPU chip at $d{=}512$, $N{=}16384$ (24.1\,GiB of compiler temporaries,
reproducible with no TPU via the compile-only probe in the repository), the
same pathology this instrument diagnosed and works around; the same cell
runs here at 245\,ms. Their reference also declines embedding tables
outright, a seam this instrument carries.
