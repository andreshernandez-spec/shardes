\subsection{Scaling to eight devices}
Figure~\ref{fig:f1} shows both scaling modes at the largest cell both
platforms ran. Strong scaling (a fixed total population split across more
devices) tracks the ideal slope for every strategy that has per-candidate
work to split; weak scaling (a fixed population per device) exceeds the
linear reference for the low-rank strategies because a larger total
population fills each device's compute better, which is utilization, not a
violation of anything. Two cells introduce stories that later figures
finish: the dense baseline's missing one-device point on the TPU is a
memory ceiling (Figure~\ref{fig:f4} maps it), and its flat line in the TPU
weak panel says more chips do not help a memory-bound strategy.

\subsection{The crossover, and the folk claim}
Figure~\ref{fig:f2} is the directional prediction of
Section~\ref{sec:contraction}, tested. B wins outright wherever every
candidate carries real contraction work (the dense and seed-regenerated
strategies), and wins harder on the TPU; for the low-rank family the two
placements sit within a few percent of each other and the sign flips inside
the grid, with A ahead at $d{=}2048$. Both halves match the prediction: B's
advantage grows with per-candidate work, and A returns where that work
vanishes; its trajectory in device count is monotone
(Figure~\ref{fig:f2b}). The folk claim resolves as true and beside the
point. Strategy A really does move only scalars, at exactly twice the naive
count (Table~\ref{tab:tb2}: the sharded evaluation makes two $4N$-byte
gathers real); strategy B moves a model-sized buffer every update and wins
much of the grid anyway, because even its most demanding measured cell asks
7.9\,GB/s of a 600\,GB/s interconnect. The claim guarded against a cost
that does not bind at this scale.

\subsection{Where the low-rank rewrite pays}
EGGROLL's motivation is explicitly GPU-shaped \citep{eggroll2025}, so the natural guess was that
a TPU, whose matrix unit makes dense multiplies comparatively cheap, would
narrow the gap or invert it. Figure~\ref{fig:f4} answers: the rewrite pays
\emph{more} on the v5e, not less, both in time (rank-1 at
$0.05$--$0.18\times$ dense against $0.13$--$0.42\times$ on the A100) and
in feasibility (the 16\,GB chip renders the dense baseline infeasible over
most of the grid, drawn as wins because feasibility is the strongest form
of paying off). The platforms move the constants, never the ordering. The
same figure prices Qiu's bet \citep{qiu2025} honestly: seed regeneration costs
$5$--$6\times$ the materializing baseline per device on the TPU against
$1.3$--$3\times$ on the GPU, and it is the only strategy that never runs
out of memory on either platform, which is precisely the
storage-for-compute trade it advertises.

\subsection{Baselines}
\input{generated/tb1}
Matched shapes, matched populations, tokens per second; nothing here claims
step-equivalence between implementations. The arms differ in antithetic
pairing, shaping and normalization details by design, and the honest unit of
comparison is throughput at the same shapes, which is how both papers report \citep{qiu2025,eggroll2025}.

Beyond parity, the measurements locate three concrete reasons to prefer the
instrument over the reference implementations, stated with their caveats.
First, none of the references shards: at eight devices the instrument reaches
$6\times$ the reference's single-chip ceiling (Table~\ref{tab:tb1b}), which
is a statement about released code, not about what the papers' internal
systems could do. Second, choices the references hardcode are measured to
matter: the contraction placement flips winner by platform
(Figure~\ref{fig:f2}), and EGGROLL's antithetic pairing is built into its
sampler, while the unpaired variant it cannot express estimates the gradient
about $1.34\times$ better per sample at matched population in the large-$N$
regime its own paper targets (the phase-0 curves behind
Figure~\ref{fig:f5}); the pairing halves factor storage in exchange, and at
small $N$ the difference is within noise, so this is a choice to expose, not
a defect. Third, the reference's rank-1 path does not compile on a 16\,GB
TPU chip at $d{=}512$, $N{=}16384$ (24.1\,GiB of compiler temporaries,
reproducible with no TPU via the compile-only probe in the repository), the
same pathology this instrument diagnosed and works around; the same cell
runs here at 245\,ms. Their reference also declines embedding tables
outright, a seam this instrument carries.

\subsection{The shaping barrier, priced}
Section~\ref{sec:design} declared one deliberate synchronization point;
here is its bill, measured in isolation across 84 cells on the TPU. The
communication half is not the story at any scale: the gather runs at
2.4--6\,$\mu$s, flat in $N$ (the payload at $N{=}2^{18}$ is 1\,MB) and
about 3\,$\mu$s dearer at $D{=}8$ than at $D{=}1$. The cost that grows is
the sort, and it is local compute paid redundantly rather than
communication: 12.1\,ms at $N{=}2^{18}$, identical to within $0.3\%$
between one device and eight, because every chip sorts the same
replicated array, about 46\,ns per member on this chip. At the
populations the source papers actually run, 30 to a few thousand, the
whole barrier is under 60\,$\mu$s against generation times in the
milliseconds. It becomes a leading term only where $N$ reaches the
rank-$r$ regime's extremes and the per-member work is cheap; at
$N{=}2^{18}$ a 12\,ms sort would rival a low-rank generation itself, and
a psum-based shaping or a sharded sort would remove it at the design
cost Section~\ref{sec:design} documents declining.

An isolation number is the barrier's worst case, since a real generation
may overlap part of the latency behind compute, so we also closed the
caveat in context: identical full generations with shaping on and off,
at the largest cells the sweep measured on this platform, differ by
41\,$\mu$s ($d{=}512$, $N{=}1024$) and 149\,$\mu$s ($d{=}2048$,
$N{=}256$) in the median, both inside their own repeat spreads. At
measured scales the barrier is statistically invisible inside a
generation; the 12\,ms sort stands as the ceiling for a population
regime where a full generation does not fit this hardware anyway.

\subsection{Ablations}
\input{generated/tb3}
Two rows earn prose. Compute dtype costs nothing: bfloat16 against float32
is within a few percent on both platforms at default matmul precision,
because the tensor core and the matrix unit already downgrade the
multiplies, so the dtype choice is about memory, never speed. Matmul
precision, by contrast, is strategy-shaped: forcing full-precision
multiplies is nearly free for the strategies whose time is the shared
base-model multiply, and costs $1.6$--$1.9\times$ exactly for the low-rank
family, whose per-candidate correction multiplies are the operations being
upgraded. A practitioner running defaults gets Figure~\ref{fig:f4}'s
speedups; our scaling sweeps, which pin full precision for cross-device
comparability, were conservative against the low-rank arms specifically.
