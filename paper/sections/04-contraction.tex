\section{The contraction question}
\label{sec:contraction}

After evaluation, every device holds fitnesses for its share of the
population, and the update $\sum_i w_i \epsilon_i$ must be assembled (the
contraction). There are two natural placements:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Strategy A: gather scalars, replicate the contraction.}
    Each device gathers every member's fitness, then regenerates all $N$
    perturbations and computes the whole contraction itself; nothing is
    combined afterwards, because every device already holds the full sum.
    Communication: scalar gathers only ($8N$ bytes as measured,
    Table~\ref{tab:tb2}; the model below explains the count). This is the
    arrangement behind the folk claim that ES only communicates scalars \citep{salimans2017}.
  \item \textbf{Strategy B: contract partially, then all-reduce.} Each device
    contracts only its own $N/D$ perturbations and the partial sums are added
    across devices (an all-reduce of a model-sized buffer). Communication:
    the full parameter count plus the same scalar gather ($4P{+}4N$ bytes as
    measured); compute per device: $D\times$ less contraction work than A.
\end{itemize}

The trade is compute against communication, so a crossover in
(population $N$, model size $d$) space is predicted rather than assumed:

The part of the model committed before any measurement is the byte
count, and the measurement corrected it. The committed prediction was $4N$
bytes of scalars for strategy A and $4P$ bytes for strategy B ($P$
parameters, float32). Table~\ref{tab:tb2} measures the payload of every
collective in the compiled update: A actually moves $8N$, a pair of
$4N$-byte gathers, because the sharded evaluation gathers the member ids
alongside the shaped fitnesses; B moves $4P{+}4N$, the same gather riding
beside its model-sized all-reduce. The refined model matches to the byte.
The correction changes no conclusion, five orders of magnitude at
$d{=}2048$ either way (2\,KB against 100\,MB), and it is reported as a
correction rather than folded silently into the prediction.
The directional prediction, also stated before measuring, follows from what
each strategy buys with its bytes: B does $1/D$ of A's contraction work, so
B should win wherever per-candidate contraction work is large (dense or
regenerated perturbations, bigger populations) and the model-sized transfer
amortizes, while A should return wherever that work is already small (the
low-rank family) and the transfer is pure overhead. No quantitative time
model was committed before the runs, and none is claimed as a prediction;
Section~\ref{sec:results} tests exactly the two statements above.
