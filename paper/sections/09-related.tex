\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{ES at scale.} \citet{salimans2017} made distributed ES credible by
sending scalar returns rather than gradients and reusing one set of
hyperparameters across tasks; ARS \citep{mania2018} and deep neuroevolution
\citep{such2017,conti2017,chrabaszcz2018} belong to the same
simplicity-first lineage. The two 2025 systems push that lineage to language
models by attacking opposite costs: \citet{qiu2025} regenerate exact noise and
fine-tune billion-parameter models with populations as small as 30, while
EGGROLL \citep{eggroll2025} factors the noise and reports roughly
$100\times$ throughput gains with populations in the tens of thousands.
\citet{cho2026reward} argue the population sizes these systems need are
largely set by reward design and normalization rather than dimension, a
complementary axis to the representation and placement questions here
(and adjacent to the shaping-degeneracy guard of
Section~\ref{sec:setup}).
Neither 2025 work measures the update-placement crossover or places both
perturbation families in one implementation across GPUs and TPUs.

\paragraph{Zeroth-order optimization for language models.} MeZO
\citep{malladi2023} also obtains gradients from forward evaluations, but its
central goal is optimizer-state memory comparable to inference, not
population-parallel throughput. It is therefore a close algorithmic sibling
with a different systems bottleneck: this paper asks how a population is
represented, sharded, and contracted once many evaluations run together.

\paragraph{Estimator design.} Analysis of Gaussian smoothing and zeroth-order
rates makes the dimension dependence explicit
\citep{nesterov2017,duchi2015,berahas2022}. Natural ES, CMA-ES, and
information-geometric optimization adapt richer search distributions
\citep{wierstra2014,hansen2001,hansen2016,ollivier2017}; this paper instead
holds the optimizer family fixed to isolate perturbation representation.
Structured orthogonal estimators and geometrically coupled Monte Carlo
predict gains from designing samples jointly
\citep{choromanski2018structured,rowland2018coupled}. Figure~\ref{fig:f5}'s
null result for orthogonal Hadamard and scrambled Sobol sampling is scoped to
the high-dimensional, low-sample, rank-shaped regime measured here; it does
not claim that coupling or quasi-Monte Carlo is ineffective in general.

\paragraph{RL stability.} The machinery ES omits has distinct lineages: target
networks address value-learning divergence
\citep{baird1995,tsitsiklis1997,mnih2015,vanhasselt2018,zhang2021target},
whereas trust regions, clipping, and reference-model penalties constrain
policy updates \citep{kakade2002,schulman2015trpo,schulman2017ppo,ouyang2022}.
Large empirical studies question which ingredients are load-bearing
\citep{engstrom2020,andrychowicz2020,orz2025}. On the ES side, reports of
drift-induced forgetting supply a cost to apparent robustness
\citep{abdi2026forgetting}, and later work finds that degradation to be
largely recoverable drift, present under RL fine-tuning as well, and
treatable with anchored regularization \citep{schweighofer2026awd}. The
exploratory stress test of Appendix~\ref{sec:stability} tests only the
policy-optimization recipe and reports collapse and drift together.

\paragraph{Distributed systems.} JAX transformations and GSPMD provide the
compiler and sharding substrate \citep{jax2018,gspmd2021}. Evosax
\citep{lange2022evosax} is the incumbent JAX ES library; rather than infer the
effect of its flattened-population interface from source alone,
Table~\ref{tab:tb1} records the resulting memory ceiling as a failure row.
The missing piece across these threads is a common, structured-perturbation
harness in which algorithm, placement, and accelerator can be varied
independently.
