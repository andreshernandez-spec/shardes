\section{Conclusion}
The usual scalar-communication account of distributed ES is true under one
update placement but incomplete as a systems model. Across the measured GPU
and TPU grids, locally contracting population shards and all-reducing a
model-sized update wins every dense and seed-regenerated cell. Repeating the
contraction after a scalar gather returns as the better placement when
low-rank perturbations make the avoided work small. The crossover moves with
the accelerator and recurs, with both signs intact, on Qwen2.5-0.5B. The
low-rank rewrite's GPU-shaped motivation also strengthens rather than inverts
on the TPU. Centered-rank shaping adds one shared fitness gather followed by a
replicated local sort; the sort, rather than communication, dominates that
barrier at extreme populations.

The validation chain gives the systems result a measured quality boundary. An
approximately square-root alignment law from a synthetic block predicts
full-rank cosine with the true teacher-forced-NLL gradient within 10\% at 0.5B
and 1.5B parameters. A low-rank correction calibrated at 0.5B predicts the
1.5B cells within 26\%, while drifting enough that it should not be treated as
scale-invariant. On decoded-reward Countdown training, a three-seed experiment
resolves no quality difference among full rank and ranks 1, 4, and 16 at equal
training samples, while rank 1 uses 44\% less steady-state time per update.
That task result is consistent with the NLL alignment evidence, not a claim
that the objectives are interchangeable. The next systems measurement is
past the single-host boundary, where the interconnect should change the
contraction question most sharply.
