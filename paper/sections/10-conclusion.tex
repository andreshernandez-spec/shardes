\section{Conclusion}
The load-bearing beliefs about distributed evolution strategies were
measurable all along, and measured, they narrow. ES moves only scalars
under one of two contraction placements, and the placement that moves a
model-sized buffer every update wins much of the measured grid anyway;
where each wins moves with the accelerator, and both crossover signs recur
on the real model. The low-rank rewrite's GPU-shaped motivation strengthens
rather than inverts on TPU. The only synchronization shared by both
placements is the fitness gather, microseconds at every measured cell; the
shaping barrier above it is a replicated local sort that reaches
milliseconds only at extreme populations; and strategy B's model-sized
all-reduce, the one synchronization beyond these, did not stop B from
winning the dense cells while its overhead sets the low-rank side of the
crossover. On a 0.5B-parameter task, the fast perturbation family shows no
detectable quality loss over three seeds at $1.8\times$ lower steady-state
cost per update. Against true gradients at 0.5B and
1.5B, the estimator law predicts full-rank cosine within 10\%, gets the
within-arm population scaling right, and transfers a 0.5B low-rank
calibration to 1.5B within 26\%, although the correction itself drifts with
scale. An exploratory stress test (Appendix~\ref{sec:stability}) collapsed one
RL recipe at its learning-rate margin while ES survived the tested
endpoints, at a measured parameter-drift cost; that is a stress test's
evidence on one task and three seeds, not a stability guarantee.
The instrument that made these comparisons commensurable is released.
The natural next measurement is past the single-host boundary, where the
contraction question changes character because the interconnect does.
